# Seeing all 330 cut on the 50 mm box — 2026-09-19

Asked for: what every library design actually looks like cut through
`fixtures/box-50.stl`. The 2026-09-15 sweep counted the pieces each cut leaves but
never drew one, and a count does not tell you whether a design is worth printing.

Two things came out of it. The pictures, and a defect they made visible.

## The defect on the wave designs

Every wave crest carried two small square steps where the rib got wider and
narrowed again. Reported while the renders were being set up, reproduced first in
the 2D tile, which is where the cut tool comes from:

| | |
|---|---|
| before | `exports/tile-waves-1-now.png` — a step on each side of every crest |
| after | `exports/tile-fixed.png` — one width all the way along |

**Cause.** `buildLibraryTile` draws a stroked subpath at its own position *and* at
every neighbouring repeat position whose copy would reach inside the box. That
exists for a good reason: the pipeline clips to the repeat box before it builds
its 3×3 neighbourhood, so a rib drawn outside the box is gone by then, and without
the copy it stops dead at the seam.

But the copy was drawn unconditionally, and a design drawn well past its repeat
usually overflows with a **lead-in, not the continuation**. waves-1 is drawn 220
units wide inside a 120-wide repeat, and over the overlap the tail drifts **1.45
units from the true continuation — 0.6 mm at a 50 mm repeat, against a 2.7 mm
rib**. So the copy did not land on the original. The two unioned into a rib that
steps wider and back twice per crest.

It was not a regression from the working tree: the same nubs are there at `HEAD`.
It came in with the library itself.

**Fix** (`src/patterns/library/tile.ts`). A neighbour copy is drawn only where it
brings material the subpath does not already put inside the box, measured on a
coverage grid read at a rib's resolution — two ribs closer than one rib width are
one rib anyway.

That threshold sounds like a judgement call and is not. Of the 292 copies the
bounding box lets through, **every one either covers nothing new or covers only
ground the original never reaches**. Nothing sits in between (`neighbour-audit.ts`).

**What it changed.** 14 designs of 330. Measured three ways:

- *Against the source drawings* (`compare-source.ts`, new). The stored SVGs in
  `C:\Code\Patterns` draw each design as an SVG `<pattern>`, which clips every copy
  to its own cell — that rendering is what the design is supposed to look like.
  Rasterised against our tile over the same field: **10 designs moved closer to
  their source, 1 moved 0.11 points further**. waves-1 went 4.47% → 0.22%
  disagreement; eyes-3, scales-1, triangles-22 and waves-2 went to 0.00%. Mean over
  all 327: 5.05% → 4.98%.
  (Most of those files are stored with their paint stripped, `stroke='none'
  stroke-width='0'`, because the site sets it from its own controls; the harness
  paints them with the widest stroke the site offers.)
- *Seam measurements* (`measure-seams.ts`, re-run). Designs losing printable design
  to the clip: 20 → **19**. Stroked designs whose ribs stop at the seam: 5 → **4**.
- *Ring parts* (`ring-sweep.ts`, re-run). **Zero** designs changed, so every
  `cutAs`/`cutInv` in `data.json` still stands.

`tests/pattern-library.test.ts` gained a test that fails without the fix: waves-1
must produce one rib per drawn path, and no point of the finished outline may sit
further from its centreline than half a rib.

### The other wave designs

"The wave patterns" is plural, so all 21 were checked against their sources.
waves-1, -2 and -3 were the nubs and are fixed. waves-8, -9 and -10 disagree with
their drawing by 7.7%, 17% and 40%, and that is **not** a defect: their lines are
finer than the nozzle at a 50 mm repeat, so the pipeline's opening removes them.
`exports/side-waves-10.png` shows it — the source is a dense moiré of hairlines,
ours keeps the ones a printer could lay down. The rest are all under 2%.

### One thing I did not fix

chevron-3 now trips the "ribs stop at the repeat edge" note, at 0.99 mm. Chasing it
showed the note is measured on a construction the app never ships — `measure-seams`
reads edge spans with `periodic: false`, while the pipeline's periodic path stitches
opposite edges into literally the same polygons. **On the tile the app actually
builds, all four flagged designs — chevron-3, scales-1, scales-3, scales-4 — have
exactly matching edges** (0.000 mm). So all four of those warnings describe the
pre-stitch geometry, not the print.

That is a pre-existing problem with what `ribBreak` measures, not something this
change caused, and fixing it properly means deciding whether the stitch inventing a
stub counts as the rib carrying across. Left alone and written down.

## The renders

`render-cut.ts` cuts each design exactly as `sweep-cut.ts` measures it — same
flatten, same layout, same tool, margin 3 mm, wall 1.6 mm, through-cut on the closed
ring of four walls — and then draws the body.

`raster.ts` is a z-buffered triangle rasteriser, no browser and no GPU. Flat shading
is exact here because every face of a cut box is planar. What makes a cut legible
rather than a grey blob is three things: true outward normals with no two-sided
flip, so the inside wall of a recess is lit as an inside wall; a depth-difference
term that darkens where material was removed; and a real floor, found by meeting
each background ray with the plane, with the geometry projected onto it along the
sun for the shadow.

Two repeat sizes, because the repeat changes the look more than anything else: at
50 mm one copy of the design fills a wall, at 25 mm the wall shows it as a pattern.

And both orientations. Which side of the drawing is material decides whether the
cut severs the box, and the app has a checkbox for it: of the 330, **81 survive a
through-cut as drawn and 77 only inverted**. For those 77 the as-drawn picture is
of a box in pieces, and the one worth looking at is the other side. A card whose
surviving orientation is not the one on screen says which way to cut it.

### Too fine to print, told apart from sparse

A box that comes back almost uncut has two quite different causes and the volume
left cannot tell them apart. plus-1 removes 1.2% of the box because it *is* one
plus sign of 1.2 mm lines per 50 mm — that is the design. geometric-4 removes
almost nothing because it is a maze of hairlines that the opening erodes to specks
before the tool is ever built. A first cut at this badge used the volume left and
mislabelled plus-1 and plus-2.

What separates them is how much is **lost**: the tile's area with the
minimum-feature filter against its area without (`fine-sweep.ts` → `fine.json`).

| of 330 | lose over half the design to the floor | over 90% |
|---|---|---|
| as drawn, 50 mm repeat | 7 | 4 |
| as drawn, 25 mm repeat | 39 | 16 |
| inverted, 50 mm repeat | 5 | 1 |
| inverted, 25 mm repeat | 18 | 9 |

That is the number the gallery shows, and it is the concrete argument for picking a
bigger repeat rather than a reject list.

Where the cut falls apart, a second render colours each loose piece, largest first.
That is the one thing a photograph of the finished print cannot show you and the
count alone does not make vivid: waves-1 at a 25 mm repeat is twelve stacked rings
that come off the plate separately.

**1320 views — 330 designs x 2 repeat sizes x 2 orientations — cut and drew with
zero errors.** 2010 renders (a coloured piece view wherever one falls apart), 300 MB.

22 views were skipped rather than cut, and each says why on its card. Twenty are
"the tile comes out empty at this size", and those are exactly the designs the
erosion sweep above puts at 100% — two measurements made independently, agreeing on
the same names. The other two are straight-lines at a 50 mm repeat, whose 20 x 80
drawing becomes a 50 x 200 mm tile that no 50 mm wall can hold a copy of.

What the cut leaves:

| | 50 mm | 25 mm | 50 mm inv. | 25 mm inv. |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| stay one printable piece | **122** | 118 | 91 | 87 |
| hang on necks too thin to print | 50 | 37 | 36 | 45 |
| visibly fall apart | 154 | 165 | 202 | 191 |
| nothing to cut at that size | 4 | 10 | 1 | 7 |

The as-drawn 50 mm column is **122 / 50 / 154**, which is what `sweep-cut.ts`
reported on 2026-09-15 to the design. Different harness, different process, same
three numbers — and the tile fix in between changed 14 designs' geometry without
moving any of them across a line.

Note that more designs fall apart inverted (202) than as drawn (154), while 77 of
them survive *only* inverted. Those are not in tension: which side holds is a
property of the individual design, not a direction that is generally safer.

### The cane weave

Added on request. It is a generator this project wrote, not a library drawing, so
it was never in the sweep above. `render-native.ts` puts a native generator through
the identical cut and the identical four views.

A library design has one knob that sets its repeat, `width`, so the gallery's two
sizes are just that knob at 50 and 25. A native has no such knob, so each one has
to say how to reach those repeats from its own defaults. The cane weave's repeat is
its cell, and the tile is `columns` of them: five cells of the default 10 mm is a
50 mm repeat, the same five at 5 mm is 25. Nothing else is touched.

| | as drawn | inverted |
|---|---|---|
| 50 mm repeat (10 mm cells) | 82 pieces | **one printable piece** |
| 25 mm repeat (5 mm cells) | 2 pieces | **one printable piece** |

Inverted is the side that holds, which is what `NATIVE_CUT_PARTS` already says for
it (`caneWeave: [554, 1]`), and the render agrees.

**The 25 mm view is worth looking at before printing one.** At a 5 mm cell the
weave stops being a weave: the generator's own `square = cell - octagon - 2*strand`
reaches zero, the paired strands merge into one, and what comes out is a field of
octagon perforations. Nothing is wrong — the maths does exactly what it documents —
but the chair-cane look needs cells nearer the default 10 mm, or thinner strands to
go with the smaller cell.

### And then all 29 of them

All but the cane weave take `width` and `height` in millimetres and fit the pattern
to them, so no per-generator table was needed after all: the fallback sets both to
the repeat and leaves everything else alone. Setting *both* is the part that
matters — `width` on its own would have left a "50 mm repeat" 60 mm tall. Feature
counts (`columns`, `order`, `depth`) stay as they are, so a smaller repeat means
finer features, exactly as it does for a library design.

**29 generators, 116 views, zero errors.** Two skips, both "the tile comes out empty
at this size" (ammannBeenker and greekKey, inverted at 25 mm). 26 of the 29 have at
least one of the four views that stays one printable piece; guilloche, hyperbolic
and truchet have none, which is what `NATIVE_CUT_PARTS` says of all three.

That table was measured independently, by `ringParts()` on a plain ring of wall
rather than by cutting this box. **25 of the 29 agree with it on which side
survives.** The four that differ — fermatSpirals, greekKey, hilbert, unicursalMaze
— differ *all in the same direction*: the box holds together where the ring says it
would not.

That one-sidedness is the point. It is precisely the effect the ring measurement
exists to remove: this box's walls are only about 44 mm between the margins, so a
repeat too tall to fit whole never gets the chance to cut anything loose and the
solid margin holds it in. A disagreement the other way — the ring surviving where
the box shatters — would have meant something was wrong. There is not one.

## Cutting each face on its own

A third axis, added on request. The two modes differ in one line — whether
`flattenPieces` unfolds the four walls into a single sheet:

- **wrapped** — one sheet, so the pattern runs continuously round the corners and a
  single margin follows the rim. This is what `sweep-cut.ts` measures.
- **per face** — each wall flattens alone, so the pattern starts again on every face
  and each gets its own margin all the way round.

**The edge width is 3 mm, the same as the wrapped mode**, deliberately rather than a
second number: it keeps the only difference between the modes the wrapping, so a
change in the parts count is attributable to that and not to a heavier frame. About
seven extrusion lines at a 0.42 mm nozzle. Two faces meeting at a corner each bring
their own margin, so a per-face box carries roughly a **6 mm solid post** at every
vertical edge — which is the point of the mode. Every panel is framed and the box
is a rigid cage rather than one wrapped skin.

`cutbox.ts` now holds the cut, the camera and the margins, shared by the library and
native sweeps, rather than a third copy of them. It was checked against the stored
rows before anything new was run: same parts, same thin count, same volume to the
decimal.

**2872 views — 359 designs x 2 repeat sizes x 2 orientations x 2 cut modes — zero
errors, nothing missing.** 47 views are skipped and say why on the card.

| whole, of 1412 comparable pairs | |
|---|---|
| both ways | 448 |
| **only per face** | **186** |
| only wrapped | 16 |
| neither | 762 |

Designs with at least one view that stays one printable piece: **253 wrapped, 281
per face**, of 359.

The biggest rescues are the designs whose cuts run straight round the ring and saw
it into bands — lusona at a 25 mm repeat goes from 26 pieces to one, stripes-2 from
20, waves-1 from 12.

### The 16 that go the other way

I had expected none, and said so after seeing the 29 native generators, where there
were none. Across the whole library there are 16, and the reasoning behind that
expectation was wrong.

The argument was that per-face only ever *adds* material, at the corners, so it
could not sever anything. But per-face does not add to the wrapped layout — it
**re-lays the pattern**, each face with its own origin and its own seam fit. The
cuts land somewhere else. In 14 of the 16 the per-face body is the *heavier* of the
two and still falls apart: diamonds-15 at a 50 mm repeat keeps 17344 mm³ against
17153 wrapped, and comes off the plate in 12 pieces rather than one.

So the two modes are not ordered. Per face wins far more often than it loses — 186
to 16 — but it is a different cut, not a safer one, which is why both are in the
gallery instead of one being preferred.
